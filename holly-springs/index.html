Overview

Why buyers look at Holly Springs

Holly Springs is a town of about 50,300 people (U.S. Census Bureau estimate, July 2025) in southwestern Wake County, between Apex and Fuquay-Varina. NC-55 runs through the middle of town as Main Street.

Two changes in the last few years affect home prices here. In September 2024, the Toll NC-540 Triangle Expressway was extended east from the NC-55 Bypass to I-40 in Garner, so Holly Springs now has a toll-road connection toward Research Triangle Park to the north and Garner to the east. Large biotech manufacturers have also opened in town.

Amgen opened a manufacturing plant in Holly Springs in January 2025 and expects about 725 employees there by 2032. Fujifilm opened a $3.2 billion manufacturing site in September 2025 and plans about 1,400 jobs by 2031. New jobs at that scale add demand for housing within a short drive.

Parts of Town

Where to look within Holly Springs

Downtown and Main Street

The original town center along NC-55. Older homes on established lots sit closest to the town's restaurants, shops, and the downtown ballpark.

Near the NC-540 Interchanges

Homes closest to the toll road have the fastest route north toward RTP and east toward Garner and I-40. Commuters usually start their search here.

Established Neighborhoods

Planned neighborhoods such as Sunset Ridge and 12 Oaks. Resale homes here usually come with mature landscaping and HOA amenities already built out.

The Fuquay-Varina Line

The southern edge of town runs into Fuquay-Varina along US-401 and NC-55. Buyers comparing the two towns can often find more land per dollar by moving a few miles south.

New Construction

Buying new construction in Holly Springs

Builders are active across Holly Springs, and the communities selling, the incentives offered, and the lots left change month to month. I keep that list current and send it on request instead of posting it here.

The builder’s agent works for the builder

The sales agent in the model home represents the builder. You can bring your own agent. Many builders require that your agent come with you or register you on your first visit, so contact me before you tour.

Compare incentives as a total

Incentives are often tied to the builder’s preferred lender or title company. Compare the full cost, including rate, closing costs, and upgrades, against an outside lender before you choose.

Read the builder’s contract

Builders usually use their own purchase agreement, and its terms on deposits, delays, and changes differ from a resale contract. Read it before you sign, and ask an attorney about anything you do not understand.

Inspect a new house anyway

A new home passes local code inspections, which check minimum standards. Hire your own inspector before drywall and again before closing, and once more before the builder warranty ends.

History

Where the name comes from

The town is named for free-flowing springs that fed a stream and a small lake, surrounded by large holly trees. The North Carolina General Assembly chartered Holly Springs as a town in 1877.

For most of the next century it stayed a small farming town along what is now NC-55. Most of its growth has come in the last thirty years, as Wake County expanded south from Raleigh, Cary, and Apex.

Living Here

Schools, parks, and things to do

Schools

  • Holly Springs High: the town's high school
  • Some Holly Springs addresses are assigned to high schools in neighboring towns. Wake County Public School System assignments change as new schools open, so verify the assignment for any address with WCPSS before you make an offer. Ratings are published on NC School Report Cards.

Parks

  • Bass Lake Park: a 1.9-mile loop trail around the lake, starting behind the nature center
  • Sugg Farm Park: town park with open space and trails
  • Jones Park and Parrish Womble Park: neighborhood parks in town
  • Carl Dean Greenway: a 1.3-mile paved walking path

Things to do

  • Holly Springs Salamanders: collegiate summer baseball team in the Coastal Plain League, playing downtown
  • W.E. Hunt Recreation Center: the town's indoor recreation center
  • Downtown Main Street: restaurants and shops along NC-55

Questions

Common questions about buying in Holly Springs

What do homes cost in Holly Springs right now?

Prices change month to month, so I send current numbers on request instead of posting figures that go out of date. Request the market report and I will send recent sales, homes for sale, and days on market for the area you are considering.

How do you get from Holly Springs to RTP and Raleigh?

Toll NC-540 connects Holly Springs north toward Research Triangle Park and east to I-40 in Garner. NC-55 runs north to Apex and Cary. Drive times depend heavily on the time of day, so test the commute from a specific address at rush hour before you buy.

Which high school serves Holly Springs?

Holly Springs High is the town's high school, but some Holly Springs addresses are assigned elsewhere. WCPSS assignments change as new schools open, so verify the assignment for any specific address with WCPSS before making an offer.

Should I look in Holly Springs or Fuquay-Varina?

The two towns border each other. Holly Springs is closer to NC-540 and the new biotech employers. Fuquay-Varina, to the south, usually offers more land for the money and has two historic downtowns. I can send recent sales from both so you can compare similar homes side by side.
