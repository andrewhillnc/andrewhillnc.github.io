Overview

What is in Providence Oaks

Providence Oaks is a subdivision inside the Fuquay-Varina town limits, in ZIP code 27526. Its plats were recorded with Wake County in 2024 and 2025. The homes that sold over the past two years are on Providence Springs Lane, Bridle Brook Way, Donnegal Farm Drive, Cedar Bird Lane, Wicklow Hall Drive, Beaminster Place, Edenberry Lane, Branthurst Drive, and Foxfield Way.

Every qualified sale here in the county file was a detached house built between 2024 and 2026, so this is a new neighborhood and most sales were recent construction. The median lot among the homes that sold was 0.17 acres. Compare these homes with other new construction homes in Fuquay-Varina on price per square foot as well as total price.

Sale data

What homes in Providence Oaks have sold for

Wake County recorded 155 qualified sales of detached houses in Providence Oaks between November 21, 2024 and September 23, 2026, covering 154 different homes (one sold twice). A qualified sale is one a county appraiser reviewed as an arm's-length transaction, so foreclosures and sales between relatives are generally left out. The median sale price was $463,000, and the median price per heated square foot was $184. Sales ranged from $370,000 to $608,500, and the middle 80 percent fell between $408,500 and $538,000. The homes ranged from 2,028 to 3,065 heated square feet, with a median of 2,448.

Before you buy

What to check on a home in Providence Oaks

Ask for the HOA documents early in your due diligence period. Read the current dues, any special assessments, what the dues cover, and the covenants on fences, sheds, and exterior changes. For a home bought from a builder, read the purchase contract and warranty before you sign, and have the home inspected even though it is new. My North Carolina due diligence guide covers how the due diligence fee, earnest money, and deadlines work.

School assignments are set by the Wake County Public School System and can change as new schools open, so look up the assignment for the specific address. School ratings and test scores are published by the state on NC School Report Cards and by GreatSchools.

Check the FEMA flood map for the address, especially for a home that backs to a creek, pond, or open space.
