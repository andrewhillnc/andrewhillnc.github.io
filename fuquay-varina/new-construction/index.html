Where builders are working

Where new homes are going up around Fuquay-Varina

Most new construction around Fuquay-Varina sits in four directions from downtown. Each one has a different mix of lot sizes, commutes, and counties, so the first decision is which side of town fits your day.

Southwest, near downtown

Serenity is a large master-planned community close to downtown Fuquay. It has several builder collections, from cottages to larger homes with three-car garages, and a section for buyers 55 and older. Living here keeps you a short drive from Main Street, the Arts Center, and Fuquay Mineral Spring Park.

South along US-401, into Harnett County

The biggest new communities with Fuquay-Varina mailing addresses are south of the Wake County line along US-401, toward Angier and Lillington. Providence Creek, Bloom, and Clarabella are here. Prices on this side usually start lower than inside Wake County, and the trade is a longer drive north and a different school system and tax bill.

East toward Willow Spring

Growth on the east side follows NC-42 and the Gold Leaf Crossing shopping area. Some communities here are inside Fuquay-Varina town limits but carry a Willow Spring mailing address, which surprises buyers searching by town name. Saunders Farm is one of them.

North, toward Holly Springs

Buyers who commute toward Holly Springs, Apex, or Cary often start on the north side of town, closer to NC-55. Newer single-family homes and townhomes here usually cost more than the Harnett County communities and give you a shorter drive north. If you are weighing the two towns, my Fuquay-Varina vs. Holly Springs comparison covers roads, downtowns, and county data on home age and lot size.

Wake County or Harnett County

A Fuquay-Varina mailing address does not tell you which county a home is in. Several of the newest communities with Fuquay-Varina addresses are in Harnett County. Two homes at the same price, a few miles apart, can come with different schools, different property taxes, and different town services.

The county sets three things that matter to most buyers:

  • Schools. Wake County homes are assigned by the Wake County Public School System. Harnett County homes are assigned by Harnett County Schools. Check the assignment for the exact address with the school system before you sign.
  • Property taxes. County tax rates differ, and a home inside town limits also pays the town rate. Compare the full yearly tax estimate, not the price alone.
  • Commute. The Harnett County communities are farther south, so drive time to Raleigh, RTP, and Cary is longer. Drive it from the community at rush hour before you choose a lot.

The parcel record shows the county and whether the lot is inside town limits. I check both before a client tours a model home, so we compare communities on the same terms.

How to buy

How to buy a new home with your own agent

Register before your first visit

Many builders require that your agent come with you or register you on your first visit to the model home. Contact me before you tour, even if you are only looking.

The builder's agent works for the builder

The sales agent in the model home represents the builder. Your own agent represents you in choosing the lot, reviewing the contract, and pushing for repairs before closing.

Compare incentives as a total

Incentives are often tied to the builder's preferred lender or title company. Compare the full cost, including rate, closing costs, and upgrades, against an outside lender before you choose.

Read the builder's contract

Builders use their own purchase agreement, and its terms on deposits, delays, and changes differ from the standard NC resale contract. Read it before you sign, and ask an attorney about anything you do not understand.

Inspect a new house anyway

A new home passes local code inspections, which check minimum standards. Hire your own inspector before drywall and again before closing, and once more before the builder warranty ends.

Look at the lot, not only the house

Ask what is planned for the land behind and beside the lot, where the drainage goes, and which streets will carry construction traffic. The Town's 2040 Land Use Plan shows what the town expects around each area.

Questions

Questions about buying new in Fuquay-Varina

Can I use my own agent to buy new construction in Fuquay-Varina?

Yes. The builder's sales agent represents the builder, and you can bring your own. Many builders require your agent to register you or attend your first visit, so contact your agent before you tour a model home.

Is every Fuquay-Varina address in Wake County?

No. Several newer communities with Fuquay-Varina mailing addresses are in Harnett County, south of the Wake County line. The county sets your school system and county tax rate, so check the parcel record for any home you are considering.

Are there 55+ new construction communities in Fuquay-Varina?

Yes. Serenity, near downtown Fuquay-Varina, has a section for buyers 55 and older. One-level ranch communities such as Kipling Village also appeal to buyers who want to avoid stairs without an age requirement.

Should I use the builder's preferred lender?

Builders often tie their best incentives to their own lender. That can be a good deal, but compare the total cost, including rate, closing costs, and any upgrade credits, against at least one outside lender before you decide.

Do I need a home inspection on a brand-new house?

Yes. Code inspections check minimum standards, not workmanship. A private inspection before drywall, another before closing, and one before the builder warranty ends catch problems while the builder still has to fix them.
