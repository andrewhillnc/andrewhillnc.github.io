How it works

How a Raleigh property tax bill is calculated

A property tax bill in Raleigh has two main parts. Wake County charges a county rate on every property in the county, and the City of Raleigh charges a city rate on property inside its city limits. Each rate is written in cents per $100 of the home's assessed value. The assessed value is the number Wake County assigned to the home at the last countywide revaluation, not the price you paid for it.

The formula is the assessed value divided by 100, multiplied by the combined rate. Wake County's tax rate page works a Raleigh example the same way, adding the county and city rates together and applying the total to the assessed value on one bill.

The bill also carries flat fees that do not depend on the home's value. Wake County adds a Residential Waste Reduction Fee of $20 per living unit. The City of Raleigh adds its own solid waste and stormwater charges. The fee amounts are small compared with the tax, but they appear on the same bill, so include them when you compare two homes.

2026 rates

The county and city rates for the 2026 bills

Both rates went up for the budget year that runs from July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027. Wake County commissioners adopted a county rate of 53.71 cents per $100 of assessed value on June 1, 2026, an increase of 2 cents from the prior year. The Raleigh City Council adopted a city rate of 37.2 cents per $100 on June 8, 2026, an increase of 1.7 cents. Together, a home inside Raleigh's city limits pays 90.91 cents per $100 of assessed value, which Wake County's tax rate page shows as 0.9091.

Estimate

What the 2026 rates mean in dollars

The table below applies the 2026 rates to three assessed values. For a home assessed at $450,000 inside the city limits, the county share is $450,000 divided by 100, times 53.71 cents, which is $2,416.95. The city share is the same value times 37.2 cents, which is $1,674.00. The combined tax is $4,090.95 before the flat fees. A home with the same assessed value outside the city limits pays the $2,416.95 county share plus the rate of whichever fire or special district it sits in, and those district rates are listed on the county's tax rate chart.

To estimate your own bill, look up the home's assessed value on the Wake County real estate search, divide by 100, and multiply by 0.9091 for a home inside Raleigh. Then add the $20 county waste reduction fee and the city's solid waste and stormwater charges. The city's proposed budget summary listed those two city charges at $342 and $102 a year for the 2026 to 2027 budget year.

Mortgage lenders usually collect property tax monthly through an escrow account, so a $4,090.95 annual tax adds about $341 to a monthly payment. When you compare two homes with different prices, compare the tax on each as a monthly number, because the gap often matters more than the difference in the list prices suggests.

City limits

Why the city limits line changes the bill

A Raleigh mailing address does not mean a home is inside the City of Raleigh. Homes with Raleigh addresses in unincorporated Wake County pay the county rate and a fire district rate instead of the city rate, and they get county or private services instead of city water, sewer, and trash collection. The two bills can differ by hundreds of dollars a year on the same assessed value, so check this before you compare homes on price alone.

The quickest check is the current tax bill. Wake County's online bill shows each taxing unit as a separate line, so a bill with a City of Raleigh line is for a home inside the city. The county's real estate search also lists the municipality or fire district for every parcel. 2027

What changes with the 2027 revaluation

Wake County revalues every property on a set schedule, and the next revaluation takes effect January 1, 2027. The county's Tax Administration will present the results to the Board of Commissioners on January 19, 2027, and owners will receive their updated assessed values by mail a few days later. The county has also announced that revaluations will move to a two-year cycle starting in 2029.

A revaluation changes the assessed value, not the tax rate. The county commissioners and the Raleigh City Council set new rates in their June 2027 budgets, after the new values are known. The bill you receive in late 2027 will use the new value and the new rates, so a 2026 bill is a guide to the current year only. If you are buying in the first half of 2027, ask the seller for the revaluation notice so you can see the new value before you set a budget.

Owners who disagree with a new value can appeal it to the county. The county's revaluation pages explain the appeal window and the evidence it accepts, and I can pull recent sales near a home to help you decide whether an appeal is worth filing.

Source: Wake County news release, September 21, 2026.

Other towns

Taxes outside Raleigh

Each town in Wake County sets its own rate on top of the county rate, and Durham, Orange, and Johnston counties set their own county rates. A home in Durham or Chapel Hill is billed by a different county with different rates and a different revaluation schedule, and homes in Clayton are billed by Johnston County. The county rate charts linked above list every town and district rate in Wake County, so compare the full combined rate for each town before you decide between them.

Common questions about Raleigh property taxes

How much is property tax on a $450,000 home in Raleigh?

When are Wake County property taxes due?
