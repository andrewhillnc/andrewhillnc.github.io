What luxury homes in Raleigh have sold for

Wake County recorded 448 qualified sales of detached homes for $1.5 million or more inside Raleigh's city limits between November 1, 2024 and October 30, 2026. That is about 6 percent of the 7,177 qualified detached home sales in Raleigh over the same period. A qualified sale is one a county appraiser reviewed as an arm's-length transaction, so foreclosures and sales between relatives are left out. Condos and townhouses are not included on this page. In the same period, 16 condos and 4 townhouses in Raleigh also sold for $1.5 million or more.

The median sale price was $1,948,250, and the median price per heated square foot was $473. Sales ranged from $1.5 million to $7 million. The median home had 4,396 heated square feet on 0.37 acres, and about 58 percent were built in 2015 or later.

Before you buy

What to check before buying a luxury home in Raleigh

On a larger or custom home, plan for a longer inspection and consider specialists for the roof, HVAC systems, pool, septic or well if the property has them, and drainage on wooded or sloped lots. My North Carolina due diligence guide explains how the fee, earnest money, and deadlines work.

If you are considering an older home inside the Beltline or a lot to build on, my Inside the Beltline guide covers historic overlay districts, infill rules, and how new builds compare with older homes on price per square foot. For homes under construction, see my new construction guide.

School assignments are set by the Wake County Public School System and can change, so look up the assignment for the specific address. School ratings and test scores are published by the state on NC School Report Cards and by GreatSchools. Check the FEMA flood map for any home near a creek, lake, or pond.

Which Raleigh ZIP codes have the most luxury home sales?
