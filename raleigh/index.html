Overview

How Raleigh is laid out

Raleigh is the capital of North Carolina and the county seat of Wake County. It has about 506,300 residents (U.S. Census Bureau estimate, July 2025).

The I-440 Beltline circles the older center of the city. I-540 runs across the north side between I-40 near Research Triangle Park and Knightdale, and Toll NC-540 continues south and east toward Holly Springs and Garner. Housing ranges from 1920s houses inside the Beltline to newer subdivisions and townhomes near the outer loop, so the right search depends on which part of the city fits your commute and budget.

Wake County records show about 119,500 single-family homes with a Raleigh mailing address. The median home was built in 1998, has about 1,860 heated square feet, and sits on 0.18 acres. About 13 percent were built in 2015 or later. Source: Wake County parcel data, checked October 21, 2026.

Parts of the City

Where to look in Raleigh

Inside the Beltline

Five Points, Hayes Barton, Drewry Hills, the Village District, Glenwood South, and downtown. Most houses date from the 1920s through the 1960s, and the lot often carries much of the value. Read my Inside the Beltline guide.

North Hills and Midtown

The mixed-use district at Six Forks Road and Lassiter Mill Road, with condos and townhomes inside it and 1960s ranches on the streets around it. Read my North Hills guide.

North Raleigh

The neighborhoods between the Beltline and I-540, along corridors such as Six Forks Road and Falls of Neuse Road, reaching toward Falls Lake.

West Raleigh

The area around NC State University and Lake Johnson, with access to I-40 and the Wade Avenue connector toward RTP and the airport.

Brier Creek

Northwest Raleigh along I-540 near Raleigh-Durham International Airport and Research Triangle Park.

South Raleigh

The south side of the city along US-401 and I-40, bordering Garner. Read my Garner guide if you are weighing the two.

Before You Buy

What to check on a Raleigh home

City limits

A Raleigh mailing address does not always mean the home is inside city limits. Check the tax bill for a City of Raleigh line, because it affects your tax rate and city services.

School assignment

Raleigh is part of the Wake County Public School System, and assignments change as new schools open. Verify the assignment for the exact address with WCPSS before you make an offer.

Flood maps

Creeks such as Crabtree Creek run through established neighborhoods. Check the FEMA flood map for any home near a creek, because flood insurance requirements can change your monthly cost.

Land value

In older neighborhoods, the county often appraises the lot for more than the house. See my land value map, or ask me to pull the county land value for any address.
