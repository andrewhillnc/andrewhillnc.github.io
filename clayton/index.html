Overview

Why buyers look at Clayton

Clayton is a town in western Johnston County, east of Garner along I-40. It had 26,307 people in the 2020 census, and recent Census estimates put it above 30,000.

Novo Nordisk already runs two manufacturing facilities in Clayton. In June 2024 it announced a $4.1 billion expansion here with 1,000 new jobs, with major construction phases expected to finish between 2027 and 2029. Grifols and Caterpillar are also large local employers.

Road names around Clayton changed in 2025. About 10 miles of the US-70 bypass between I-40 (Exit 309) and the US-70 Business interchange became I-42. Old US-70 Business became US-70 again, named Clayton Boulevard near the county line, and NC-42 in this area was renumbered NC-36. Older listings and maps may still use the former names.

Parts of Town

Where to look within Clayton

Downtown and Main Street

The Clayton Historic District is on the National Register of Historic Places. Older homes near Main Street are closest to downtown restaurants and The Clayton Center.

Golf and Club Communities

Clayton has three golf clubs: Riverwood Golf & Athletic Club, Pine Hollow Golf Club, and Neuse Country Club. Riverwood is also a planned neighborhood built around its course.

Along I-42 and US-70

The employer corridor, including Novo Nordisk and Grifols. Homes near these interchanges have the shortest drive to work for people at those plants.

West Toward Cleveland and I-40

The unincorporated Cleveland area sits between Clayton and Garner along I-40 and NC-36. Addresses here may carry a Clayton or Garner mailing address while sitting outside both town limits, so check before you assume town services. My McGee’s Crossroads guide covers the I-40/NC-210 side.

Neighborhoods

Neighborhoods buyers ask about in Clayton

These are the communities that come up most often when buyers search Clayton. Several sit along the Neuse River. I send current sales for any of them on request.

Flowers Plantation

A master-planned community of about 3,000 acres east of Clayton with townhomes, single-family homes, custom homes, and some gated sections. It has about 20 miles of paved trails and its own YMCA.

Riverwood Athletic Club

Homes of about 1,200 to 3,600 square feet built in several phases. HOA dues include the athletic club, which has indoor and outdoor pools and a fitness center. Trails connect to the Neuse River Greenway.

Riverwood Golf

A separate Riverwood neighborhood built around an 18-hole course, with lots averaging about an acre, many on the course. It has a clubhouse and community pool.

Glen Laurel

Custom homes built mostly from 2004 to 2018 around Neuse Country Club, about five minutes from downtown Clayton. Some lots back to the golf course, and some have private river docks.

Portofino

A gated community beside the Neuse River with homes built from 2008 to 2023 on wooded lots of half an acre to over an acre. It has an equestrian center, trails, a fishing pond, and river canoe access.

Wellesley

Mostly custom homes on standard and estate-size lots near the center of Clayton, with a pond and walking trail, pool, and tennis courts.

Knolls at the Neuse

Ranch and two-story homes on varied lots, with a racquet club at the entrance and trails to the river and the Neuse River Greenway.

Academy Pointe

Newer low-maintenance homes on compact lots within walking distance of downtown Clayton. HOA dues include a membership at Fred Smith athletic clubs.

History

From Stallings’ Station to Clayton

The town grew along a road cut around 1770 by Governor Tryon’s troops marching from New Bern to Hillsborough. When the North Carolina Railroad arrived nearly a century later, the depot was in the home of Sarah Stallings, and the stop was called Stallings’ Station.

The name changed to Clayton after about three years, and the town was incorporated in 1869. Cotton, watermelons, and tobacco were the early crops. Eight Clayton properties are now on the National Register of Historic Places, including the Clayton Historic District and the Cleveland School.

Living Here

Schools, parks, and things to do

Schools

  • Clayton High and Cleveland High serve the Clayton area.
  • Clayton is in Johnston County Public Schools, not the Wake County system. Verify the assignment for any address with Johnston County Public Schools before you make an offer.

Parks and trails

  • Clayton River Walk on the Neuse: 4 miles of paved trail, part of the Mountains-to-Sea Trail
  • Sam’s Branch Greenway: 1.25 miles, connecting to the River Walk; together they reach Falls Lake Dam in Raleigh, about 32 miles north
  • Clayton Community Park and East Clayton Community Park

Getting around

  • I-40 west to Garner and Raleigh
  • I-42 (the former US-70 bypass) east toward Smithfield and Goldsboro
  • US-70 / Clayton Boulevard and NC-36 (formerly NC-42) for local trips

Common questions about buying in Clayton

What do homes cost in Clayton right now?

Prices change month to month, so I send current numbers on request instead of posting figures that go out of date. Request the market report and I will send recent sales, homes for sale, and days on market for the area you are considering.

How do you get from Clayton to Raleigh?

I-40 runs west from Clayton through Garner to Raleigh. From the I-40/I-42 interchange in Garner, Toll NC-540 runs west toward Holly Springs and RTP. Test the commute from a specific address at rush hour before you buy.

Why do some listings say NC-42 and others NC-36?

In 2025 NCDOT renumbered NC-42 in this area as NC-36 and turned the US-70 bypass into I-42. The road is the same; the sign changed. Older listings and maps may still use the old numbers.

Should I look in Clayton or Garner?

Clayton is in Johnston County, with its own school system and property tax rate, and is close to the Novo Nordisk and Grifols plants. Garner is in Wake County and closer to Raleigh. I can send recent sales from both so you can compare similar homes side by side.
